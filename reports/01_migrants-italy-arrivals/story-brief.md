# Story Brief — Migrant arrivals to Italy over time

## Journalistic angle
How have migrant arrivals to Italy changed over the long run — not just the last decade,
but as far back as reliable data allows? The analysis examines whether the current flows
are historically exceptional, comparable to past peaks, or part of a longer structural trend.
The goal is to put the present political moment in a quantitative historical context.

## Editorial hook
The immigration debate is very heated in Italy politically right now. Public discourse
frequently invokes emergency framing and peak-crisis comparisons without checking whether
the numbers actually support them. A long-run data portrait anchors the debate in evidence.

## Primary audience
General audience with no statistical background. Statistical concepts must be explained
inline; the narrative register should be accessible and avoid jargon.

## Scope
### In scope
- Migrant arrivals to Italy over the longest available historical series
- National-level data (Italy as a whole)
- Trend analysis: direction, magnitude, and any identifiable structural breaks

### Out of scope
- Regional or sub-national breakdown of arrivals
- Distinction between legal channels and irregular arrivals (unless data clearly separates them)
- Comparison with other European countries (may appear as context but is not the primary angle)
- Integration, labour market, or demographic outcomes

## Null hypothesis

### Falsifiability condition
Not applicable. This is a descriptive investigation: the story is the data, whatever
direction the trend takes. A flat, rising, or falling long-run series are all publishable
findings. No hypothesis is pre-registered.

### Methodological floor
The analysis must cover at least 20 years of data. If the longest available series
covers fewer than 20 years, the scope limit must be disclosed explicitly in the report.

### Framing commitments (in lieu of null hypothesis)
- The full available time series will be shown, not a selected window chosen after
  seeing the data.
- The political annotation layer will not be used to imply causation in the narrative.
- If Italy's trend diverges from the European backdrop, both directions will be reported
  with equal prominence — the analysis does not pre-assume Italy is an outlier.

### Scope trigger
If the available data series is too short or too fragmented to support a meaningful
long-run portrait, the analysis is scoped down to what the data allows, with explicit
disclosure of what is missing.

### Pre-registration date
18 May 2026 — recorded before any data is downloaded.

> **Note:** This analysis is descriptive. No hypothesis was pre-registered in the
> scientific sense. Results should be interpreted as a data portrait, not as
> confirmatory evidence for any causal claim.

## Clarifications

### Geographic scope
Italy is the primary subject. Europe (EU countries) serves as backdrop for comparison.
No specific country subset defined — all available European entities will be included
where data allows, with Italy highlighted.

### Time period
As far back as reliable data allows. End point: most recent available data.
No fixed start year — determined by source series coverage.

### Comparison baseline
Italy's own historical trend is the primary line. European data (EU average or comparable
countries) appears as contextual backdrop, not as an equal subject of analysis.

### Unit of analysis
Both absolute counts (number of persons) and rates per 100,000 population.
Absolute counts show scale; rates enable fair cross-country comparison.

### Audience calibration
General audience. Basic statistical terms (average, trend, peak) are acceptable
with brief inline explanations. No jargon beyond that.

### Political annotation (C4)
The timeline of Italian Prime Ministers from 1994 onwards will be annotated visually
on the chart — as labelled bands or markers — to give political context to the trend.
This is a visual device only. No analytical claim is made about the relationship between
government composition and migration flows. A disclosure note will appear on the chart.
